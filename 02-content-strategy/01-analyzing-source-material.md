# Analyzing Source Material

Before writing anything, read the source material with a decomposition mindset. You're
looking for natural boundaries — the seams where one concept ends and another begins.

## First Pass: Identify the Skeleton

Read the table of contents, chapter headings, and section titles. These reveal the
author's intended structure. Ask:

- What are the major themes or domains?
- Which concepts depend on which others?
- Where does the source naturally cluster related ideas?

Map these to potential skillbook sections. A source chapter often becomes a skillbook
section, but not always — sometimes you'll merge thin chapters or split dense ones.

## Second Pass: Find the Atoms

Within each major theme, identify the atomic concepts — ideas that can stand alone as
a single page. Look for:

- **Definitions** — a term or concept explained in depth
- **Procedures** — a step-by-step process
- **Rules** — requirements, constraints, or specifications
- **Classifications** — taxonomies, tiers, categories
- **Examples** — case studies, worked problems, illustrations

Each of these is a candidate for a single page.

## Third Pass: Map Dependencies

Draw the dependency graph. Which concepts require understanding other concepts first?
This determines both section order and reading flow within sections.

Common patterns:
- **Linear** — Chapter 2 requires Chapter 1 (tutorials, textbooks)
- **Hub-and-spoke** — One core concept with independent subtopics (reference manuals)
- **Layered** — Foundation → intermediate → advanced (certifications)

The dependency graph directly informs your section ordering — see
[Section Organization](03-section-organization.md).

## Output: The Section Plan

After three passes, you should have:
1. A list of 4-8 sections with descriptive names
2. A rough list of pages within each section
3. A dependency ordering for the sections
4. Notes on which source content maps to which pages

Share this plan with the human before writing. It's much easier to reorganize a plan
than to restructure finished pages.
